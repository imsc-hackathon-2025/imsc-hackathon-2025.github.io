Dataset & Challenge

Climate Intelligence & Earth System Modeling Challenge

Challenge Overview

Develop advanced AI models to understand and predict climate patterns using multi-modal Earth observation data. Create novel deep learning architectures that can process satellite imagery, atmospheric data, ocean measurements, and ground-based sensors to provide insights into climate change impacts and future projections.

Research Objectives

  • Develop novel multimodal fusion architectures for Earth system modeling
  • Create interpretable AI models for climate pattern analysis
  • Achieve state-of-the-art performance on climate prediction benchmarks
  • Design scalable systems for processing petabyte-scale Earth observation data
  • Contribute research-grade innovations with publication potential

Dataset Structure

satellite_imagery/

Size: 2.5 TB | Images: 15M+

Multi-spectral satellite imagery from multiple missions (Landsat, Sentinel, MODIS)

RGB + NIR bands Temperature data Cloud masks Land cover labels Change detection

atmospheric_data/

Size: 850 GB | Records: 50M+

Global atmospheric measurements and reanalysis data

Pressure levels Wind vectors Humidity profiles Precipitation Aerosol data

ocean_measurements/

Size: 1.2 TB | Records: 25M+

Ocean temperature, salinity, and current measurements

SST anomalies Ocean currents Salinity profiles Sea level data Ice coverage

ground_stations/

Size: 650 GB | Records: 100M+

High-resolution ground-based measurements and station data

Temperature time series Precipitation gauges Wind measurements Solar radiation Soil moisture

Research Challenges

Multimodal Fusion

Develop novel architectures to effectively combine satellite imagery, atmospheric profiles, and ground measurements at different spatial and temporal resolutions.

Interpretability

Create interpretable models that can explain climate phenomena and provide insights into physical processes driving observed patterns.

Scale & Efficiency

Handle massive-scale data processing while maintaining computational efficiency and enabling real-time inference capabilities.

Uncertainty Quantification

Develop methods to quantify and communicate uncertainty in climate predictions and handle missing or noisy data.

Evaluation Criteria

Research-grade evaluation standards

Evaluation Process

Projects undergo rigorous peer review similar to academic conferences. Evaluation includes technical merit, novelty, experimental rigor, and potential impact. Top submissions may be recommended for publication in partner journals.

Research Evaluation Framework

Technical Innovation

30%
  • Novelty of proposed methods and algorithms
  • Technical depth and sophistication
  • Algorithmic contributions and improvements
  • Computational efficiency and scalability

Scientific Rigor

25%
  • Experimental design and methodology
  • Statistical analysis and significance testing
  • Proper baselines and ablation studies
  • Reproducibility and code quality

Impact & Significance

25%
  • Potential for real-world climate impact
  • Advancement of scientific understanding
  • Broader applicability and generalization
  • Societal and policy implications

Research Communication

20%
  • Quality of research presentation
  • Clear articulation of contributions
  • Professional documentation and writing
  • Effective visualization and explanation

Research Deliverables

Research Paper Requirements

  • 8-page research paper in conference format (ACM/IEEE style)
  • Abstract, introduction, related work, methodology
  • Experimental setup, results, and statistical analysis
  • Discussion of limitations and future work
  • Complete bibliography with relevant citations
  • Supplementary materials and appendices

Code & Data Requirements

  • Complete, well-documented source code
  • Reproducible experimental pipeline
  • Containerized environment (Docker/Singularity)
  • Comprehensive dataset documentation
  • Performance benchmarks and evaluation scripts
  • Version control with detailed commit history

Research Presentation Requirements

  • 15-minute research presentation
  • Technical motivation and problem significance
  • Methodology and experimental design
  • Results analysis and interpretation
  • Contributions and future research directions
  • Q&A session with expert panel
